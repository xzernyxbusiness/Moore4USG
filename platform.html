Community

Give students more places to gather and more freedom to host the people they care about.

The problem

Community life at UChicago is crucial to our experience as college students, and some parts of it still need work. Two places to start are the spaces where we spend time outside class and the rules about who we can host in our dorms.

[Add a real example: a survey result, a space that's always full or closed, or a story from a student you talked to.]

What I'll do
  • Improve and expand third spaces, the places that aren't class, the library, or your own room, where students can gather. [Name the specific spaces, buildings, or hours you'd target.]
  • Raise the dorm guest limit from 2 guests to 4. [Confirm the current policy wording and which housing types it covers.]
Who decides

What USG can do directly: survey students, write and pass a resolution, and publish the results.

What needs the administration: changing space use and hours, and changing the guest policy. I'll bring student data and a written proposal to [Housing & Residence Life / Campus & Student Life: confirm which offices own each decision].

Timeline

Done by Spring Quarter.

  • Autumn: survey students and map existing spaces.
  • Winter: submit proposals to the administration.
  • Spring: changes in place.
How you'll know it worked
  • The guest policy change is published.
  • [Number] new or improved gathering spaces are open to students.
  • A follow-up survey shows [your target, e.g. a majority of residents] are satisfied.

Access

Make everyday necessities easier to afford and easier to reach.

The problem

On many fronts, students face issues with access to basic necessities. Printing, laundry, and menstrual products are small costs that add up, and they weigh most on students with the least financial cushion.

[Add numbers if you have them: what students pay per quarter for laundry or printing, or what the current assistance programs cover.]

What I'll do
  • Introduce more robust and accessible financial assistance programs for printing and laundry.
  • Extend Maroon Dollars so they can be used for laundry and printing.
  • Include menstrual products in dorm restrooms as part of wellness needs.
Who decides

What USG can do directly: gather cost data, collect student support, and fund or run a pilot if the budget allows.

What needs the administration: changing how Maroon Dollars work, laundry and printing services, and restroom supplies. I'll bring the data to [the offices that run Maroon Dollars, laundry, printing, and dorm facilities: confirm names], and propose a pilot in a few dorms if a full rollout isn't possible at first.

Timeline

Done by Spring Quarter.

How you'll know it worked
  • Maroon Dollars are accepted for laundry and printing.
  • Menstrual products are stocked in [number or "all"] dorms.
  • I publish how many students use the assistance programs each quarter.

Transparency

Show students where the money goes.

The problem

Where does tuition money go? How are USG and the University spending their money? Students pay for both and deserve clear answers.

[Add what's missing today, e.g. what the current tuition information leaves out, or what students can't see about Finance Committee spending.]

What I'll do
  • Introduce a more in-depth online tuition breakdown.
  • Provide receipts for Student Government Finance Committee purchases.
Who decides

What USG can do directly: publish receipts for Finance Committee purchases. [Confirm this is allowed under USG's rules, and whether it needs a bylaw change.]

What needs the administration: publishing a detailed tuition breakdown. I'll make the case with student support and [name the office that publishes tuition information].

Timeline

Done by Spring Quarter.

How you'll know it worked
  • Receipts are posted within [number] days of each Finance Committee purchase.
  • A tuition breakdown page is live and linked from the USG site.
