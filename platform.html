Community

Give students more places to gather and more freedom to host the people they care about.

The problem

Community life at UChicago is crucial to our experience as college students, and some parts of it still need work. Two places to start are the spaces where we spend time outside class and the rules about who we can host in our dorms. Especially during the first few weeks of school, we are limited to who we can hang out with and where on campus. It can be difficult to find areas inside to hang out with friends you make from other dorms. The 2 guest policy specifically limits us from hanging out with friends from other dorms inside our living spaces, forcing us to find other spaces outside to congregate, or leading to the hassle of bringing in other members of our dorm to scan people in.

This problem is especially relevant to underclassmen. Upperclassman that live off campus are able to support apartment gatherings where there is no restriction on friends who are able to congregate together indoors. For underclassmen, where do we all meet together to hangout outside of the libraries, classrooms, or outside? When we go out to fraternities (where we can all hang out in theory), there is the added risk of safety when getting back home. At other Universities, dorm parties are more prevalent and safer alternatives than strictly outdoor hangouts. Our dorm culture is already strong, and alleviating the issue of finding a place to hang out with friends from other dorms can be alleviated with the following fixes.

What I'll do
  • Improve and expand third spaces, the places that aren't class, the library, or your own room, where students can gather. Current projects in Ida Noyes are already working to push for this change. This can be expanded to other buildings, however. Around campus, there are many hallways that feel empty or bland. There are many spaces that feel like you are only meant to pass through, not linger. Adding more furniture to these spaces, and encouraging third spaces in underutilized spaces will help to alleviate the current lack of third spaces.
  • Raise the dorm guest limit from 2 guests to 4. According to the Housing Website, "Residents may host no more than two guests at a time, regardless of guest affiliation. To request an exception to this policy, the host is responsible for ensuring that the Guest Exception Request Form is completed at least forty-eight (48) hours prior to the visitation period." While this makes sense for overnight non-UChicago guests, UChicago students should have the ability to have 4 UChicago-affiliated guests. Increasing the limit from 2 to 4 will help to ensure that you can make and hang out with friends from other dorms more comfortably, with less stress of safety concerns of outdoor spaces at night, or being pressured to only socialize with non-dorm friends at places like fraternity parties.
Who decides

What USG can do directly: survey students, write and pass a resolution, and publish the results.

What needs the administration: changing space use and hours, and changing the guest policy. I'll bring student data and a written proposal to the Housing & Residence Life Department, and advocate to administration using student polling.

Timeline

Done by Spring Quarter.

  • Autumn: survey students and map existing spaces.
  • Winter: submit proposals to the administration.
  • Spring: changes start to take place.
How you'll know it worked
  • The guest policy change is published.
  • multiple new or improved gathering spaces are open to students.
  • A follow-up survey shows many are satisfied.

Access

Make everyday necessities easier to afford and easier to reach.

The problem

On many fronts, students face issues with access to necessities. Printing, laundry, and menstrual products are small costs that add up, and they weigh most on students with the least financial cushion.

Currently, laundry costs students $3.00 per cycle ($1.50 to wash, $1.50 to dry). This cost quickly adds up, and for some students this can be more of a burden than a basic necessity.

What I'll do
  • Introduce more robust and accessible financial assistance programs for printing and laundry.
  • Extend Maroon Dollars so they can be used for laundry and printing.
  • Include menstrual products in dorm restrooms as part of wellness needs in each dorm bathroom.
  • Re-evaluate placement of gendered bathrooms in dorms to be more accessible to everyone.
Who decides

What USG can do directly: gather cost data, collect student support, and fund or run a pilot if the budget allows.

What needs the administration: changing how Maroon Dollars work, laundry and printing services, and restroom supplies. I'll bring the data to the Housing & Residence Life department, as well as Dining, and propose a pilot in a few dorms if a full rollout isn't possible at first.

Timeline

Done by Spring Quarter.

How you'll know it worked
  • Maroon Dollars are accepted for laundry and printing.
  • Menstrual products are stocked in most dorms.
  • I publish how many students use the assistance programs each quarter.

Transparency

Show students where the money goes.

The problem

Where does tuition money go? How are USG and the University spending their money? Students pay for both and deserve clear answers.

The current website has a simple breakdown of the attendance at this University. I think that it should go into more detail.

What I'll do
  • Introduce a more in-depth online tuition breakdown.
  • Provide receipts for Student Government Finance Committee purchases.
Who decides

What USG can do directly: publish receipts for Finance Committee purchases. Provide outlines for how we would plan to allocate all funds ahead of time.

What needs the administration: publishing a detailed tuition breakdown. I'll make the case with student support and the Financial Aid Department.

Timeline

Done by Spring Quarter.

How you'll know it worked
  • Receipts are posted within 72 hours of each Finance Committee purchase.
  • A tuition breakdown page is live and linked from the USG site.
